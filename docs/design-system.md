# PaperSwipe Visual Standard

PaperSwipe uses **luxury editorial intelligence** as its visual direction. It
should feel like a high-end technology magazine crossed with a trusted research
instrument—not a generic SaaS dashboard and not a neon science-fiction demo.

## Non-negotiable principles

1. **Editorial hierarchy.** Instrument Serif carries ideas and emotional
   emphasis; DM Sans carries controls, metadata, and evidence. Large titles and
   small technical labels should create deliberate contrast.
2. **Dominant ink, warm paper, precise signal color.** Near-black is the field,
   warm off-white is reserved for the paper itself, cyan marks relevance, and
   orange marks novelty or momentum. Accent color must communicate meaning.
3. **One memorable object per screen.** The paper card owns Today, the
   constellation owns Signal Map, and the tilted product card owns the landing
   hero. Secondary decoration must never compete with these objects.
4. **Asymmetry with discipline.** Overlap, tilted cards, and off-grid labels are
   encouraged when they strengthen hierarchy. Content alignment and reading
   rhythm remain precise.
5. **Motion confirms state.** Reveal, hover, swipe, and progress motion should
   explain entry, action, or completion. Decorative motion is removed under the
   user's reduced-motion preference.
6. **Mobile is a composition, not a shrink.** The product becomes a single-hand
   card ritual, navigation moves to the thumb zone, and nonessential evidence
   moves below the primary decision.

## Release gate

Every visual release must preserve the paired fonts, the four semantic colors,
visible keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, 390px and 1440px layouts, the
Signal Fingerprint, and the two map visualizations. A real browser review of the
product hero, one complete action, Signal Map, pricing, and the mobile card is
required before deployment.
